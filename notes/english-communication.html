English for Work & Life

494+ polished phrases, power words and idioms for fluent speakers who want to sound sharper at work — and more expressive with friends and family. Every phrase shows the plain version it replaces.

How to Use This Page

The idea

You're already fluent. This page is about range and precision — swapping the default phrase for the one that sounds considered, confident and senior.

Each card shows the plain line you probably use today, then the upgrade. Learn the swap, not the definition.

  1. Pick 3 swaps a day from one section — or follow the 30-day plan at the end.
  2. Say them aloud in your own context: your project, your manager, your client.
  3. Deploy one in a real conversation today. Retrieval in context is what makes words stick.
  4. Listen for them in podcasts, earnings calls and senior colleagues — noticing reinforces memory.
  5. Revise on Day 1, 3, 7 and 21. Spaced repetition moves words into active vocabulary.
Labels on every phrase: Formal clients, seniors, emails Informal friends, teammates Both safe anywhere
Remember: polish isn't about big words. It's about the precise word, delivered without hesitation.

1. Openers That Set the Tone

The principle

Fluent speakers often default to "Hi, how are you?". Polished speakers open with warmth plus a specific detail — it signals you were paying attention.

Greeting with presence

Instead of Nice to meet you

Great to put a face to the name

Both

You've emailed/called before; now meeting in person.

“Great to put a face to the name — your notes on the audit were invaluable.”

Use it: Client visits, offsites

Instead of Thanks for coming

I've been looking forward to this

Formal

Shows genuine interest in the meeting.

“Thanks for making time — I've been looking forward to this conversation.”

Use it: Interviews, senior meetings

Instead of I've heard about you

Your reputation precedes you

Both

People speak highly of you (warm, slightly playful).

“Ah, Meera — your reputation precedes you. The pricing model was yours, wasn't it?”

Use it: Meeting someone well known

Instead of How are you?

How's the week treating you?

Both

A livelier "how are you".

“Morning, Kabir! How's the week treating you so far?”

Use it: Start of calls

Instead of What are you doing these days?

What's been occupying most of your time lately?

Both

Invites a real answer, not "fine".

“What's been occupying most of your time lately — still the migration?”

Use it: Networking, 1:1s

Instead of Long time no see

It's been too long

Both

Warm reunion line.

“Ishaan! It's been too long — you've moved to strategy, I hear?”

Use it: Old colleagues

Answering "How are you?" with substance

Instead of I'm busy

Busy in the best way

Both

Lots of work, all of it meaningful.

“Busy in the best way — we're two weeks from launch.”

Use it: Any check-in

Instead of I'm fine

Use it: Casual chats

Instead of I'm very good

Introducing yourself with impact

Instead of I work on…
