Detailed syllabus

Phase I · General Awareness 80 Q · 25 min
  • Current affairs of the last 6 months, national and international
  • Banking and financial awareness: RBI functions, monetary policy tools (repo, CRR, SLR, OMO), financial regulators
  • Government schemes, Union Budget, Economic Survey highlights
  • Important reports, indices, committees, summits, appointments, awards
  • Static GK linked to economy: national institutions, international bodies (IMF, World Bank, BIS)
Phase I · Reasoning 60 Q · 45 min
  • Puzzles and seating arrangement (linear, circular, floor, box, scheduling)
  • Syllogism, inequalities, coding-decoding, blood relations, direction sense
  • Input-output, data sufficiency, order and ranking, alphanumeric series
  • Critical reasoning: statement-assumption, argument, course of action, inference
Phase I · Quantitative Aptitude 30 Q · 25 min
  • Data interpretation: tables, bar, line, pie, caselets, missing DI
  • Simplification and approximation, number series, quadratic equations
  • Arithmetic: percentage, ratio, averages, profit-loss, SI/CI, time-work, time-speed-distance, boats, mixtures, partnership, ages
  • Probability, permutation and combination, mensuration
Phase I · English Language 30 Q · 25 min
  • Reading comprehension (economy and banking themes are common)
  • Cloze test, fillers, error spotting, sentence improvement
  • Para jumbles, word usage, phrase replacement, vocabulary
Phase II · Paper I · Economic & Social Issues 100 marks
  • Growth and development: measurement of growth, national income and per capita income, poverty alleviation and employment generation, sustainable development and environment
  • Indian economy: economic history, changes in industrial and labour policy, monetary and fiscal policy, priorities and recent reforms, inflation, public finance, Union Budget
  • Globalisation: opening up of the Indian economy, balance of payments, export-import policy, international economic institutions (IMF, World Bank, WTO), regional economic cooperation
  • Social structure in India: multiculturalism, demographic trends, urbanisation and migration, gender issues, social justice (positive discrimination for the underprivileged), social movements, Indian political system, human development, social sectors (health, education)
Phase II · Paper II · English (Writing Skills) 100 marks
  • Essay writing (often on economy, society, technology)
  • Précis writing
  • Reading comprehension with written answers
  • Assessment is on expression, clarity and understanding of the topic; answers are typed
Phase II · Paper III · Finance & Management 100 marks
  • Financial system: regulators (RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA), RBI functions, money markets, capital markets, bond and forex markets, financial inclusion, alternate sources of finance
  • Banking: Basel norms, NPAs and resolution (IBC), priority sector lending, payment systems and fintech, digital currency, cybersecurity
  • General topics: risk management in banks, derivatives, changing landscape of the banking sector, corporate governance, Union Budget concepts, inflation and its measurement
  • Management: nature and scope, role of a manager, leadership theories and styles, human resource development, motivation (Maslow, Herzberg, McGregor), morale, communication, conflict, change management, corporate governance and ethics, organisational behaviour
DEPR · Economics Phase I + Phase II
  • Microeconomics: consumer theory, production, market structures, welfare economics
  • Macroeconomics: national income, money and banking, inflation, monetary and fiscal policy, growth models
  • International economics, public finance, environmental economics
  • Quantitative methods: calculus, linear algebra, statistics, econometrics
  • Indian economy: current developments, RBI and financial sector
DSIM · Statistics Phase I + Phase II
  • Probability, random variables and distributions
  • Sampling theory, estimation, testing of hypotheses
  • Linear models, regression, multivariate analysis, time series
  • Econometrics, numerical analysis, basic computer and data-science concepts

How to prepare

  • Phase I is only a filter, but the overall and sectional cut-offs are tight: aim to clear GA and Reasoning with a buffer, since they carry 140 of 200 marks.
  • Reasoning gets 45 minutes for 60 questions; drill puzzles and seating daily using the Reasoning Aptitude notes on this site.
  • Use the Calculation Practice page for speed in DI and simplification; Quant has only 25 minutes for 30 questions.
  • For GA, follow RBI press releases, the Monetary Policy Statement, the Annual Report and the Financial Stability Report; keep a 6-month current-affairs log.
  • Start Phase II (ESI and F&M) in parallel with Phase I: the gap between Phase I and Phase II was only 6 weeks in 2026.
  • For the descriptive parts, practise typing structured answers of 200–300 words to time; use the Verbal Ability notes to tighten grammar before Paper II essay and précis practice.
  • Read the Economic Survey and Union Budget summaries for ESI, and RBI reports and circulars for Finance.
  • For the interview, prepare your graduation subject, home state economy and current RBI policy debates.

How to prepare

  • Phase I Paper 1 has 80 questions in 60 minutes with a low 30% cut-off, so the real filter is Paper 2 and the 40% aggregate: spend most of your time on the stream paper.
  • General stream: the Paper 2 syllabus is the same in both phases, so study it once in depth. Start with Accountancy, Costing and Companies Act (these are numerical and static), then Finance and Economics, and keep monthly notes on SEBI and RBI circulars.
  • IT stream: Phase II gives 180 minutes for 100 code-reading MCQs. Practise predicting output and spotting bugs in Java/C++/Python by hand. The DSA, Java, Databases and Backend notes on this site cover most of the syllabus, and you can pick up networking and shell basics alongside them.
  • For Paper 1, use the Quantitative Aptitude, Logical Reasoning and Verbal Ability notes and the Calculation Practice page. Twenty questions per section in 60 minutes rewards accuracy more than speed.
  • Practise typing: the English paper (essay, précis, comprehension) is typed in 60 minutes. Write one full set a week and time it.
  • Phase II is about 6 weeks after Phase I, so finish the Phase II descriptive and stream preparation before Phase I and use the gap only for mocks and revision.
  • For the interview (15% of final marks), prepare SEBI's structure and functions, recent market reforms (T+1 settlement, F&O curbs, SME IPOs, investor protection) and the subjects you studied, and link them to the stream you applied for.

How to prepare

  • ESI and ARD make up 80 of the 100 Prelims merit marks and the whole of Mains Paper II, so study them once for both stages, in depth, with descriptive answers in mind.
  • For ARD, build short notes around the official syllabus headings (agronomy, soil and water, allied sectors, climate change, rural development schemes) and add current data from the Economic Survey, the Union Budget and NABARD's annual report.
  • For ESI, cover poverty, inflation, employment, social justice and the IMF/World Bank/WTO, using the Economic Survey chapters and government scheme pages for current figures.
  • The qualifying sections need only modest scores (2025 UR cut-offs were 1–6 marks), so revise with the Quantitative Aptitude, Logical Reasoning and Verbal Ability notes and the Calculation Practice page without over-investing. Try to finish the qualifying sections in about 45 minutes so that most of the 120 minutes goes to the 100 merit-section questions.
  • Type one set of 15-mark and 10-mark ESI/ARD answers every week (introduction, data, schemes, way forward) and one full English paper (essay, précis, letter) in 90 minutes.
  • Mains comes about 5 weeks after Prelims, so start descriptive practice before Prelims.
  • For the interview, prepare NABARD's role (refinance, RIDF, supervision of RRBs and cooperatives, FPOs, SHG-bank linkage), your home state's agriculture profile and your graduation subject.
