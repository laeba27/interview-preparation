Selection process

  1. Tier-I · CBE100 questions, 200 marks, 1 hour · screening only; separate cut-offs for JSO/SI Gr-II, for AAO/AAO and for all other posts
  2. Tier-II · Paper-I (all candidates)Maths, reasoning, English and GA count for merit · Computer Knowledge Test and DEST are qualifying only, with higher standards for some posts
  3. Tier-II · Paper-II / Paper-IIIPaper-II (Statistics) only for JSO and SI Gr-II; Paper-III (Finance & Economics) only for AAO/AAO · counts for merit for those posts
  4. Option form, FRTA and slidingYou fill in post preferences online; allocation is by Tier-II merit and preference; you attend identity verification at the regional office and choose Fix or Float
  5. Final result and document verificationUser departments check documents; posts like Inspector (CBIC/CBN), SI (CBI/NIA/NCB) also carry physical and medical standards, checked after selection

Exam pattern

What changed recently: in CGLE 2026 each Tier-I section has its own 15-minute timer and you can no longer move between sections; the two Tier-II Paper-I sections now also have internal timers; and Paper-III (Finance & Economics) is back because the Level 8 AAO/AAO posts are in this cycle (CGLE 2025 had no Level 8 posts and no Paper-III).

Detailed syllabus

Quantitative Aptitude / Mathematical Abilities Tier-I 25 Q / 50 marks · Tier-II 30 Q / 90 marks
  • Arithmetic: number system, decimals and fractions, percentage, ratio and proportion, square roots, averages, simple and compound interest, profit and loss, discount, partnership, mixture and alligation, time and distance, time and work
  • Algebra: school identities, elementary surds, graphs of linear equations
  • Geometry: triangle centres, congruence and similarity, circles (chords, tangents, angles), common tangents
  • Mensuration: triangles, quadrilaterals, regular polygons, circles, prism, cone, cylinder, sphere, hemisphere, cuboid, pyramid
  • Trigonometry: ratios, degree and radian, standard identities, complementary angles, heights and distances
  • Data and statistics: histogram, frequency polygon, bar and pie charts; Tier-II adds mean, median, mode, standard deviation and simple probability
General Intelligence and Reasoning Tier-I 25 Q / 50 marks · Tier-II 30 Q / 90 marks
  • Semantic, symbolic/number and figural analogy, classification and series
  • Coding-decoding, numerical and symbolic operations, word building, trends
  • Venn diagrams, syllogism and drawing inferences, critical thinking
  • Non-verbal: space orientation and visualisation, paper folding and punched holes, figure completion, embedded figures, mirror/water images
  • Indexing, address and date-city matching, roll number and centre-code classification
  • Emotional and social intelligence questions
English Comprehension / English Language Tier-I 25 Q / 50 marks · Tier-II 45 Q / 135 marks
  • Spot the error, fill in the blanks, sentence improvement, active/passive voice, direct/indirect speech
  • Synonyms, antonyms, homonyms, spelling errors, idioms and phrases, one-word substitution
  • Shuffling of sentence parts and of sentences in a passage, cloze passage
  • Comprehension: in Tier-II, three or more passages (one simple story-based, others on current affairs or editorials)
General Awareness Tier-I 25 Q / 50 marks · Tier-II 25 Q / 75 marks
  • History, culture, geography and economy of India and its neighbours
  • Polity and general policy, scientific research, everyday science
  • Current events: appointments, awards, schemes, sports, summits, reports and indices
  • Static GK: books and authors, important days, organisations and headquarters
Computer Knowledge Test Tier-II 20 Q / 60 marks · qualifying
  • Computer basics: organisation of a computer, CPU, input/output devices, memory and its organisation, backup devices, ports, Windows Explorer, keyboard shortcuts
  • Software: Windows, MS Word, Excel and PowerPoint
  • Internet and e-mail: browsing and searching, downloading and uploading, managing e-mail, e-banking
  • Networking and cyber security: devices and protocols, threats (hacking, viruses, worms, Trojans) and prevention
Data Entry Speed Test (DEST) Tier-II · 15 min · qualifying
  • Typing an English passage of about 2,000 key depressions (about 8,000 key depressions per hour) on the computer
  • Judged on errors against the passage; accuracy matters more than raw speed
  • PwBD-OH candidates can be exempted with a medical certificate, except for posts that need computer proficiency or DEST (Tax Assistant in CBDT excepted)
Paper-II · Statistics 100 Q / 200 marks · JSO and SI Gr-II only
  • Collection, classification and presentation of data; frequency distributions
  • Central tendency, partition values, dispersion, moments, skewness and kurtosis
  • Correlation and regression (incl. rank, multiple and partial correlation for three variables)
  • Probability theory, Bayes' theorem; random variables; binomial, Poisson, normal and exponential distributions
  • Sampling theory and techniques; statistical inference (estimation, Z, t, chi-square and F tests); ANOVA
  • Time series analysis and index numbers
Paper-III · General Studies (Finance and Economics) 100 Q / 200 marks · AAO/AAO only
  • Finance and Accounts (80 marks): financial accounting concepts and GAAP; double entry, journal, ledger, trial balance, bank reconciliation, rectification of errors; final accounts; capital vs revenue; depreciation; inventory valuation; non-profit accounts; bills of exchange; self-balancing ledgers
  • Economics and Governance (120 marks): CAG and Finance Commission; micro economics (demand, supply, elasticity, consumer behaviour, production and cost, market forms)
  • Indian economy: sectors, national income, population, poverty and unemployment, infrastructure, reforms since 1991
  • Money and banking: RBI and monetary/fiscal policy, banks/RRBs/payments banks, budget, fiscal deficit, balance of payments, FRBM Act 2003; IT in governance

How to prepare

  • Tier-I is now four locked 15-minute sections: practise sectional mocks with a strict timer so that one slow section does not cost you the others. Tier-I only shortlists, so aim to clear the cut-off comfortably rather than chase a perfect score.
  • Maths decides most ranks: work through the Quantitative Aptitude notes on this site, then add geometry, trigonometry and mensuration (big in SSC and not covered by bank syllabi), and do the Calculation Practice page daily for speed.
  • Tier-II English carries 135 of the 390 merit marks: use the Verbal Ability notes and read one editorial a day for the comprehension passages; revise idioms, one-word substitutions and spellings from a fixed list.
  • For reasoning, use the Logical Reasoning notes, and practise non-verbal items (paper folding, embedded figures, mirror images) that bank exams do not test.
  • Tier-II negative marking is a full mark on 3-mark questions: attempt only what you are fairly sure of, and track your accuracy in every mock.
  • Do not ignore the qualifying parts: practise DEST typing for 15 minutes a few times a week and revise the computer basics list; failing either removes you whatever your other scores.
  • If you want JSO/SI Gr-II or AAO, prepare Statistics or Paper-III from the start, because those papers also count for merit for those posts.
  • Fill the option form carefully: allocation follows your preferences strictly, and posts with physical standards (CBIC Inspectors, SI in CBI/NIA/NCB) reject you if you cannot meet them.

How to prepare

  • Paper-II English is 200 of the 320 merit marks, so English decides selection: use the Verbal Ability notes on this site and do a timed set of 25–30 grammar and vocabulary questions every day.
  • Paper-I is now only 15 minutes per part: build speed with sectional mocks and daily drills on the Calculation Practice page and the Tables & Speed Tricks notes.
  • Cover SSC-style maths (geometry, trigonometry, mensuration) from the Quantitative Aptitude notes, and the reasoning topics from the Logical Reasoning notes.
  • Start physical training now, not after the Paper-I result: the 1.6 km run in 6.5 minutes (men) and 800 m in 4 minutes (women) eliminate the most people. Train three to four days a week, and practise long jump and high jump technique.
  • Check your height, chest and eyesight early; vision is tested without glasses, and flat foot or knock knee lead to rejection at the medical.
  • Delhi Police aspirants (men): get the LMV licence for both motorcycle and car before the PE&MT date.
  • If you are in the 2025 cycle, keep revising English and the new Paper-II topics while waiting for the PET result and the Paper-II date.

  • Shorthand in English (Pitman or similar) or Hindi, reaching 80 wpm (Grade D) or 100 wpm (Grade C) over a full 10 minutes
  • Transcription on a computer within 40–65 minutes, depending on grade and language, with few errors (omissions, substitutions, spelling)
  • Hindi transcription must be typed in Mangal font, so practise with a Hindi keyboard layout that works with it

How to prepare

  • English is 100 of the 200 marks and the first tie-breaker, so give it about half your study time. Use the Verbal Ability notes for grammar and vocabulary and practise 100-question sets in 60 minutes.
  • With separate timers from 2026, you cannot borrow time between parts. Practise reasoning and GA as 30-minute blocks of 50 questions (about 36 seconds per question).
  • For reasoning, work through analogies, series, coding and figure questions from the Logical Reasoning notes. Number-series and arithmetical-reasoning questions get faster with the Calculation Practice page and the Tables & Speed Tricks notes.
  • GA is 50 marks with only 0.25 negative marking: revise static GK (history, polity, geography, science) and 6 months of current affairs, and attempt the questions you are fairly sure of.
  • Start shorthand months before the CBE. Build dictation speed step by step (60, 80, then 100 wpm) with 10-minute passages, and transcribe every passage on a computer against the clock (40–50 minutes in English).
  • If you take the test in Hindi, practise transcribing in Mangal font now. Transcripts in any other font will not be evaluated in the 2026 skill test.
  • Apply for both grades if you are eligible: Grade D has far more posts (1,129 vs 146 in 2026) and a lower dictation speed.
